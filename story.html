Our story

And then we remembered a trip

Why convenience, not automation, is what we're actually building.

In October 2024, on a family trip that happened to be arranged through a travel agent, something shifted in how we thought about travel. From the moment we booked our tickets, the trip was already being taken care of: flights, hotels, and transfers were sorted before we'd even left home.

Once we landed, the coordination didn't stop. Every logistical decision (where to go next, how to get there, what needed to be booked next) was handled for us in the background, without us having to chase any of it down ourselves.

What stood out most was the day-to-day experience. Every morning, a message laid out the full plan for the day: where we were going, timings, nearby food options, transport details, anything we needed. The same plan went to our driver, so everyone stayed aligned. There was nothing to decide, search for, or coordinate in the moment. We simply got into the car and enjoyed the trip.

And that was the moment something clicked. The product isn't a set of destination suggestions or a ready-made itinerary. It's the entire experience, from the moment someone starts wondering where to go to the moment they're back home. Not generic, not the same for everyone, but shaped entirely around what that one traveler wants from that one trip.

We picked the pace, the places, what we wanted to do each day. The agent never decided our trip for us. They just removed the work of running it.

We didn't realize it then, but that's almost exactly what we'd later hear, again and again, from other travelers. They don't want less control. They want less work. The research, the comparisons, the coordination: that's the part people want off their plate. The decisions that make a trip feel like their trip are the ones they want to keep.

That's the bar we're building TravelWithMe toward: not deciding for you, just doing the work around it.
