Founder's story

And then I remembered a trip

Why convenience, not automation, is what I'm actually building.

One family trip, in October 2024, completely changed the way I think about travel. From flight bookings and hotel reservations to local transfers, everything was coordinated for us, from the planning stage all the way to the day we flew back home. Every logistical decision, where to go next, how to get there, what needed to be booked, was handled in the background.

What stood out most was the day-to-day experience. Every morning, a message laid out the full plan for the day: where we were going, timings, nearby food options, transport details, attraction tickets for the day, and anything else we needed to know. The same plan went to our driver, so everyone stayed aligned. There was nothing to decide, search for, or coordinate in the moment. We simply got into the car and enjoyed the trip.

We still picked the pace, the places, what we wanted to do each day. It was all done in the open, and every decision stayed ours. Whoever was coordinating it never decided our trip for us; they simply removed the work of running it.

And that was the moment something clicked. The product isn't a set of destination suggestions, itineraries, or bookings. It's the entire experience: the convenience of having it all in one place, the transparency of knowing what's happening at every step, and never losing control over the decisions that are yours to make.

That's almost exactly what I'd later hear, again and again, from other travelers. They don't want less control. They want less work. The research, the comparisons, the coordination: that's the part people want off their plate. The decisions that make a trip feel like their trip are the ones they want to keep.

That's the bar I'm building TravelWithMe toward: not deciding for you, just doing the work around it.
